A field guide by [@VibeMarketer_](https://x.com/vibemarketer_) worth reading if you use Hermes for content: [How to Build a One-Person Media Company With Hermes Bots](https://x.com/vibemarketer_/status/2093330541177352217).

The claim is that writing was never the bottleneck — finding the idea, developing the angle, and distributing it without repeating the same post on five platforms is. The answer is a team of six specialized Hermes bots, each owning one decision:

- **Signal Scout** finds ideas with a reason to exist now
- **Researcher** builds the source-bound evidence package
- **Content Strategist** picks the one strong angle
- **Long-form Writer** produces the flagship piece
- **Distribution Bot** rebuilds the idea natively for each platform
- **Editor** checks the whole package before it reaches you

What makes it worth your time is the architecture, not the roster. The author routes every bot through a shared content brain (an Obsidian vault holding voice, audience, proof, and platform playbooks) and gives the work a durable path with Hermes's profiles, bot mode, and kanban. Every handoff is an inspectable record, and the human stays at the editorial boundary — approving angles, claims, and posts, and teaching the system through every yes and no.

The part I'd underline for content work: repurposing fails when you treat formatting as distribution. An article does not become an X post because you cut 1,500 words. It becomes one because the distribution bot asks what part of the idea fits each platform's consumption pattern, and gives someone a reason to see it even if they already saw the flagship.

The post pairs cleanly with the workflow I've been running: put the roles on the board, keep the handoffs inspectable, and let the human be the final gate.
